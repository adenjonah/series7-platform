- **muni-L5-05** · Municipal securities · Expert / trap · incorrect · 14s
  - Which of the following statements regarding the Municipal Securities Rulemaking Board (MSRB) is TRUE?
  - Key: MSRB rules are enforced by FINRA for broker-dealers and by the bank regulators (OCC, Federal Reserve, FDIC) for bank dealers
  - Chose: MSRB rules apply to municipal advisors but not to bank dealers
  - Why: The MSRB writes rules but has no enforcement or examination power; FINRA enforces for broker-dealers, the bank regulators for bank dealers, and the SEC over all (05-municipal.md §3.1). - **reg-L5-02** · Laws, underwriting, trading · Expert / trap · incorrect · 33s
  - A customer enters an order to sell 100 XYZ at 45 STOP, limit 45. After entry the tape shows: 45.10, 45.00, 44.90, 44.80, 44.95, 44.85. The order is:
  - Key: Elected but not executed
  - Chose: Executed at 44.95

- **fnd-L5-05** · Foundations & economics · Expert / trap · incorrect · 27s
  - A corporation declares a regular quarterly cash dividend with a record date of Thursday, June 11, 2026. What is the LAST day an investor can buy the stock in a regular-way trade and still receive the dividend?
  - Key: Wednesday, June 10
  - Chose: Tuesday, June 9
  - Why: Under T+1 (FINRA Rule 11140 as amended 2024-05-28) the ex-dividend date for a regular cash dividend is the record date itself, so a purchase on Wednesday, June 10 settles on the record date and earns the dividend; buying on June 11 is ex-dividend. - **reg-L5-05** · Laws, underwriting, trading · Expert / trap · incorrect · 22s
  - An investment adviser registered under the Investment Advisers Act acquires, in the ordinary course of business for its clients' accounts, 6% of a company's registered common stock on March 10. It has no intention of influencing control of the company. What must it file, and by when?

  - Chose: Schedule 13G, within 10 calendar days of March 10
  - Why: Schedule 13D is for owners above 5% who intend to influence control. - **eq-L5-03** · Equity & analysis · Expert / trap · incorrect · 37s
  - All of the following transactions change a corporation's working capital EXCEPT:
  - Key: Paying off accounts payable with cash
  - Chose: Declaring and paying a cash dividend to common stockholders
  - Why: Working capital = current assets - current liabilities; paying a current liability with cash reduces both sides equally, so the difference is unchanged. Declaring and paying a cash dividend and buying equipment for cash both send cash out with no offsetting drop in current liabilities, so working capital falls; a long-term bond issue brings in cash against a long-term liability, so it rises. See 03-equity.md §3.13.4. *(M18)*
