- **opt-L2-04** · Options · SIE level · incorrect · 67s
  - ABC stock is trading at 56. An ABC Oct 50 call is in the money for:
  - Key: both the holder and the writer of the call
  - Chose: neither party, until the stock rises above 50 plus the premium
  - Why: Moneyness compares stock to strike (56 − 50 = 6 in the money), ignores the premium, and is identical for buyer and writer; a short call is in the money whenever the stock is above the strike (06-options.md §3.3). *(M13)*

  - Key: lower annual expenses, because no manager is being paid to pick stocks
  - Chose: a guaranteed return equal to the index
  - Why: Index funds are passive, with low turnover and the lowest expense ratios; they still carry full market risk and only approximate the index after costs. See §3.2 (fund types; expense ratio). *(M14)*

- **acct-L2-03** · Accounts, suitability, margin · SIE level · incorrect · 61s

- **debt-L1-02** · Debt securities · Everyday literacy · incorrect · 24s
  - Two years ago Dan bought a bond that pays 4% interest a year. Similar new bonds now pay 6%. If Dan tries to sell his bond today, what happens to its price?
  - Key: It falls, because a buyer can get 6% elsewhere and will only take Dan's bond at a discount
  - Chose: It stays the same, because the issuer still pays exactly 4%
  - Why: A bond is a fixed stream of payments; when the going rate rises, that fixed stream is worth less, so the price must fall until its yield matches the market (04-debt.md §2 items 3-4, §3.4.5). *(M12)*
