## Confident-but-wrong at or below level (3)

- **eq-L1-04** · Equity & analysis · Everyday literacy · incorrect · 17s
  - A company has both preferred stock and common stock. Compared with a common stockholder, a preferred stockholder generally:
  - Key: Receives a fixed dividend before common stockholders receive anything
  - Chose: Has more votes at shareholder meetings
  - Why: Preferred stock pays a fixed dividend and has priority over common for dividends and in liquidation, but usually has no vote and no share in earnings growth. See 03-equity.md §3.2.1. *(M11)*
- **muni-L1-01** · Municipal securities · Everyday literacy · incorrect · 50s
  - A city wants to build a new high school that will serve residents for the next 40 years, but the cost is far larger than one year's tax collections. Why would the city issue bonds rather than pay for the school out of a single year's budget?
  - Key: Bonds spread the cost over many years so the taxpayers who use the school over its life share in paying for it
  - Chose: Bond interest is paid by the federal government, making borrowing free for the city
  - Why: A municipality borrows to fund large, long-lived capital projects and repays principal and interest from future taxes or revenues over the bond's life (05-municipal.md §3.1, §3.2.1). Bonds are real debt; the tax exemption is on the investor's federal income tax, not a federal subsidy of the city's interest. *(M26)*
- **fnd-L1-03** · Foundations & economics · Everyday literacy · incorrect · 58s
  - You own a bond that pays $40 a year on every $1,000 invested. Newly issued bonds of the same quality now pay $60 a year per $1,000. If you want to sell your bond today, what must happen?
  - Key: You must accept less than $1,000, so the buyer's return catches up to what new bonds pay
  - Chose: You will get exactly $1,000, because the bond's face value never changes
  - Why: Nobody pays full price for $40 a year when $60 a year is available, so the price of the existing bond drops until its yield matches the market — the price/yield seesaw. Face value is what the issuer repays at maturity, not what a buyer pays today. (02-foundations.md §3.H.4, §3.G.3) *(M7)*

## “I don't know” at level (5)

- **opt-L1-02** · Options · Everyday literacy · didn't know · 29s
  - Maria sells (writes) an option contract to Dan and collects a $300 premium. Dan later decides not to use the contract and lets it expire. What happens to the $300?
  - Key: Maria keeps it; the premium is hers whether or not Dan ever exercises
  - Why: The premium is the price of the right and is paid to the writer up front; it is never refunded. If the option expires unused, the writer's profit is the entire premium (06-options.md §3.1, §3.4). *(M13)*
- **muni-L1-02** · Municipal securities · Everyday literacy · didn't know · 24s
  - A friend tells you that the interest on her city's bonds is "tax-free." In everyday terms, what does that mean for her?
  - Key: She does not owe federal income tax on the interest payments she receives from the bond
  - Why: Municipal bond interest is generally exempt from federal income tax under IRC §103, but a capital gain from selling the bond above cost is fully taxable, which is the classic trap (05-municipal.md §3.3, §4). *(M12)*
- **acct-L1-04** · Accounts, suitability, margin · Everyday literacy · didn't know · 1s
  - An investor's stock portfolio held at a brokerage firm falls 40% during a market downturn. He asks whether SIPC will reimburse his losses. The BEST response is that SIPC:
  - Key: protects customers only when a brokerage firm fails and their cash or securities are missing, not against market losses
  - Why: SIPC is a non-profit membership corporation (not a government agency) that steps in when a broker-dealer becomes insolvent and customer assets are missing, up to $500,000 per separate customer including $250,000 cash. It never covers a decline in the market value of securities. *(M15)*
- **muni-L1-05** · Municipal securities · Everyday literacy · didn't know · 38s
  - A city bond and a corporate bond of similar credit quality mature on the same date, but the city bond pays a noticeably lower interest rate. What is the MOST likely reason investors accept the lower rate on the city bond?
  - Key: Investors keep the city bond's interest free of federal income tax, so a lower rate leaves them with just as much after tax
  - Why: Because municipal interest is federally tax-exempt, investors compare after-tax returns, so muni yields sit below comparable taxable yields before tax (05-municipal.md §3.3, §3.5 yield relationships). Municipal bonds are not federally guaranteed. *(M12)*
- **muni-L1-03** · Municipal securities · Everyday literacy · didn't know · 14s
  - A state highway authority sells bonds to build a new toll road and promises to repay bondholders only from the tolls the road collects. Who is ultimately paying back these bonds?
  - Key: The drivers who use the road, through the tolls they pay
  - Why: This is a revenue bond, backed only by the revenues of the specific facility and not by the issuer's taxing power (05-municipal.md §3.2.2). If tolls fall short, the bondholders rather than the taxpayers bear the loss. *(M26)*
